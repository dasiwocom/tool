Why links must be escaped

A URL only allows a limited set of characters directly. Spaces, Chinese text, quotes, angle brackets, and # either truncate the request or get misinterpreted by the server. Encoding replaces those characters with their safe form; decoding restores them.

What this tool does

Open the URL encoder and paste text to encode or decode it. Two granularities are available, matching the two JavaScript functions:

  • Component encoding (encodeURIComponent) — also escapes separators like / : ? &; use this inside a query parameter.
  • Whole-URL encoding (encodeURI) — keeps separators intact and escapes everything else; use it on a complete address.

How Chinese text is handled

Unicode characters are converted to their UTF-8 byte stream, then each byte is written as a %XX escape. For example, the two characters for "tool" become %E5%B7%A5%E5%85%B7. As long as both ends speak UTF-8, Chinese travels safely in links.

Common mistakes

  • Decoding is not decrypting — anyone can strip the percent escapes to recover the original.
  • Don't whole-encode query parameters — it masks & and =, and the server can no longer split them.
  • A space may be + — in forms and query strings a space encodes as +, while in paths it is %20.
